# KYC and AML Customer Due Diligence Policy

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This policy sets Meridian Bank's requirements for knowing its customers (KYC), customer due diligence (CDD), enhanced due diligence (EDD), sanctions screening and suspicious activity reporting, in order to prevent money laundering and terrorist financing. It applies to every customer and every product.

## 2. Customer Identification Program

### 2.1 Minimum Information

Before opening any account, Meridian Bank must obtain and verify the customer's full legal name, date of birth, residential address and a government-issued identification number.

### 2.2 Verification Methods

Identity is verified through a valid government photo ID plus either an electronic check against an approved identity data provider or a utility bill dated within the last 3 months. Remote onboarding requires a liveness check.

### 2.3 Outcome

A CIP outcome is "pass", "refer" or "fail". Credit applications can only proceed with a "pass". Credit analysts cannot override a "refer" or "fail" outcome; only Financial Crime Compliance can resolve a referral.

## 3. Customer Risk Rating

Each customer receives an AML risk rating of low, medium or high at onboarding, based on occupation, source of funds, geography, expected activity and product.

### 3.1 Enhanced Due Diligence

Enhanced due diligence is mandatory for:

- Politically exposed persons (PEPs), their family members and close associates.
- Customers with residence or significant ties to high-risk jurisdictions on the internal high-risk country list.
- Customers in cash-intensive businesses.
- Customers whose expected monthly cash deposits exceed USD 20,000.

EDD requires documentation of source of wealth and source of funds and approval of the relationship by a Financial Crime Compliance officer. PEP relationships also require approval by a senior manager.

### 3.2 Periodic Review

- High-risk customers: KYC review every 12 months.
- Medium-risk customers: every 3 years.
- Low-risk customers: every 5 years.

A review is also triggered by a material change in activity or an adverse media hit.

## 4. Sanctions Screening

All customers and related parties are screened against sanctions lists at onboarding and daily thereafter. A potential match must be reviewed within 24 hours. Accounts with a confirmed match are frozen immediately and reported according to the applicable sanctions regulation.

## 5. Transaction Monitoring and Reporting

### 5.1 Suspicious Activity

Employees who suspect money laundering must report it to Financial Crime Compliance through the internal referral form the same business day. Compliance must decide whether to file a suspicious activity report (SAR) and file it within 30 calendar days of initial detection.

### 5.2 Tipping-Off Prohibition

Employees must never inform a customer, directly or indirectly, that a referral or SAR has been made or is being considered.

### 5.3 Cash Transaction Reports

Cash transactions above USD 10,000 in a single business day, including multiple transactions that together exceed that amount, must be reported in a currency transaction report.

## 6. Interaction with Credit Decisions

AML and KYC requirements are not waivable through the credit exceptions process (MB-POL-005). A credit application from a customer with a pending KYC review cannot be approved until the review is complete.

## 7. Record Keeping

KYC records and transaction records are kept for at least 5 years after the end of the customer relationship.
